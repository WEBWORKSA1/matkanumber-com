## What numerology claims

Numerology assigns numbers to names and dates and says those numbers reveal character or forecast events. The best-known Western version is credited to followers of Pythagoras. Indian traditions link numbers to planets, and Chinese tradition treats 8 as fortunate because its spoken form resembles the word for prosperity.

## What the evidence says

There is no reliable evidence that numerology predicts anything. When its claims have been tested, the results match chance. That should not be a surprise: the number you get depends on the alphabet you spell your name in and the calendar you date your birth by, and both are human conventions.

## Why it feels accurate

- **Vague statements fit everyone.** "You value independence but sometimes need support" is true of almost anybody. Psychologists call this the Barnum effect.
- **We remember hits and forget misses.** A lucky day on the 7th gets remembered. The ordinary 7ths do not.
- **Numbers really do carry meaning in culture.** Seven days, twelve months, 108 beads on a mala. Familiar numbers feel significant because we meet them so often.

## Lucky numbers and draws

This is where harmless fun turns expensive. A "lucky" number has exactly the same chance in a fair draw as every other number. Birth dates, dreams, vehicle plates and name numbers do not change the odds, and anyone selling lucky numbers for a draw is selling nothing.

There is one practical effect, and it works against you. In prize pools shared between winners, popular choices such as birthdays and 7 mean more people to split with if those numbers come up.

## Enjoy it for what it is

Treat numerology like a horoscope column: a conversation starter and a piece of cultural history.
