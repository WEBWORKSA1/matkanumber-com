If betting on numbers is costing you money you cannot spare, or taking over your thoughts, you are not alone, and it is not a failure of character. These games are built to keep people playing.

## Signs worth taking seriously

- Betting more to win back what you lost
- Borrowing, or using rent, fees or household money to play
- Hiding how much you play from family
- Feeling restless or irritable when you try to stop
- Believing the next number will fix everything

One or two of these is reason enough to act.

## First steps

1. **Tell one person you trust.** Secrecy keeps the habit going.
2. **Cut the supply.** Delete the apps, leave the tip groups and channels, and block the numbers of agents.
3. **Put a barrier between you and money.** Ask a family member to hold cards or set low daily transfer limits with your bank.
4. **Write down the real total.** Add up a month of stakes and returns. The [odds calculator]({{ '/tools/odds-calculator.html' | relative_url }}) shows why the total can only go one way.
5. **Fill the time.** Urges fade in 20 to 30 minutes. Walk, call someone, or do anything that uses your hands.

## Free support lines

- **India:** Tele-MANAS, the national tele-mental-health service, on **14416**. Counsellors are available in many languages.
- **United Kingdom:** National Gambling Helpline run by GamCare, **0808 8020 133**, and [gamcare.org.uk](https://www.gamcare.org.uk/).
- **United States:** National Problem Gambling Helpline, **1-800-GAMBLER**, and [ncpgambling.org](https://www.ncpgambling.org/).
- **Anywhere:** Gamblers Anonymous runs free peer meetings in many countries, in person and online.

If you are thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number now.

## If you are worried about someone else

Do not pay their debts without a plan, because it usually funds the next round. Do stay calm, talk about what you have noticed, and help them make the first call. The helplines above support family members too.
