A giveaway is only as good as the trust in its draw. These steps work for a social media giveaway, a school event, a club night or an office prize.

## Before you open entries

1. **Write the rules down.** Who can enter, how, the closing date and time (with time zone), the prize, how many winners, and how and when the winner will be told.
2. **Keep entry free.** Charging for a chance to win a prize turns a giveaway into a lottery or raffle, which is regulated or banned in many places.
3. **Say how the draw will be done.** For example: "Winners will be drawn at random using the Matka Draw tool on MatkaNumber.com and the draw record will be posted here."

## Building the entry list

4. **Close on time.** Take entries only up to the deadline.
5. **Clean the list.** Remove entries that break the rules and decide in advance whether duplicates count.
6. **Freeze the list.** Save a copy before the draw. If privacy allows, publish it, or publish entry numbers instead of names.

## The draw

7. **Draw live or record your screen** if the prize is significant.
8. **Paste the list into the [Matka Draw]({{ '/tools/matka-draw.html' | relative_url }})**, set the number of winners and draw once. Do not redraw because you would have preferred another result.
9. **Draw reserves at the same time** in case a winner cannot be reached.

## Afterwards

10. **Publish the draw record.** It shows the time, the number of entries, the winners and a fingerprint of the entry list that proves the list was not edited later.
11. **Contact winners privately** and give a clear deadline to respond.
12. **Keep your records** for a few months in case of questions.

## Common mistakes

- Picking "at random" by scrolling and stopping. People are not random.
- Redrawing quietly when the winner is a friend or a competitor. If certain people are excluded, say so in the rules.
- Asking winners for payment to release a prize. Real prizes never work that way, and scammers copy giveaways using exactly this trick. Warn your audience.
