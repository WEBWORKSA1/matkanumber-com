## The one idea you need

Every bet has an **expected value**: the average result per round if you could play it forever. You find it by multiplying each possible outcome by its chance and adding up.

Take a single-digit draw. Ten digits, one winner, so your chance is 1 in 10. Suppose a win pays 9 times your stake.

- 1 time in 10 you receive 9.
- 9 times in 10 you receive 0.
- Average received per 1 staked: 0.1 × 9 = **0.90**.

You hand over 1.00 and get back 0.90 on average. The missing 0.10 is the organiser's cut, and it comes out of every round whether you win that round or not.

## It is the same at every level

| Draw | Chance | Fair payout | Typical payout | Player gets back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One digit (0 to 9) | 1 in 10 | 10× | 9× | 90% |
| Two digits (00 to 99) | 1 in 100 | 100× | 90× | 90% |

Payouts differ from one organiser to another, but they are always set below the fair figure. That is the business model.

## What a year looks like

Stake 10 a day for a year on a two-digit draw paying 90×. You stake 3,650. You can expect about 3.65 wins, worth about 3,285. The expected loss is 365. Some years you will be ahead and some years far behind, but the average sits there, and the longer you play the closer you get to it. ## Why tips, charts and "fixed numbers" cannot help

A fair draw is independent: the pot does not remember yesterday. A chart of old results therefore contains no information about the next one. Anyone selling a "sure" number is in one of two positions. Either the draw is honest, and they are guessing with the same 1-in-100 chance as you. Or the draw is rigged, and the people who rigged it have no reason to share the answer for a small fee.

Tip sellers survive on a simple trick. Give ten different digits to ten groups of people and one group always wins. That group comes back and pays more.

## Doubling up does not fix it

Raising your stake after each loss (a "martingale") changes how the losses arrive, not their size. You win small amounts often and lose everything occasionally. Each round still returns 90%, so the total still falls.

## The takeaway

When the payout is below the odds, the only winning move is not to play. If you want the fun of a draw, [run a free one]({{ '/tools/matka-draw.html' | relative_url }}) for friends with a prize nobody paid to enter for.
