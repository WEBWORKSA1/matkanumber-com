Reading the result

A game is fair when the payout matches the odds: a 1-in-100 result should pay 100 times the stake. Real number games pay less, and the gap is the organiser's profit. The example loaded above is a two-digit draw (1 in 100) that pays 90 times the stake. The return to player is 90%, so on average 10 of every 100 staked is gone for good.

The green part of the bar is what comes back to players as a group. The red part is what the organiser keeps. Press "Simulate" a few times: a short run can end ahead, which is what keeps people playing, but the more rounds you add, the closer the result sits to the expected loss.

The formula

Expected return = chance of winning × payout. With a chance of 1/100 and a payout of 90, that is 0.9, or 90%. Expected loss = total staked × (1 − expected return). Nothing about past results, charts or "lucky" numbers changes either figure.
