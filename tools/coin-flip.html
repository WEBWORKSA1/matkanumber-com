Heads or tails, with proof

Each flip is an independent 50:50 event. The running count under the coin shows how the share of heads wanders early on and drifts toward 50% as flips add up. It also tracks your longest streak, because streaks surprise people. In 100 fair flips, a run of five or more of the same side is more likely than not.

The lesson in a streak

After five heads in a row, the chance of heads on the next flip is still exactly one half. The coin does not know what came before. Believing that tails is now "due" is called the gambler's fallacy, and it is the mistake that number-guessing schemes rely on.
