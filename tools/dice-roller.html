About the dice

Each die is fair: every face has the same chance on every roll. With one six-sided die each face comes up 1 time in 6. With two dice the totals are not equally likely. There are 36 possible pairs, and six of them add up to 7, which makes 7 the most common total. Only one pair gives 2 and only one gives 12.

Try this experiment

Roll two dice 36 times and tally the totals. You will rarely get exactly six 7s, but 7 will usually lead. Roll 360 times and the pattern becomes much clearer. That is the law of large numbers: chance is noisy in the short run and steady in the long run.

Which dice can I use for board and role-playing games?

All the standard ones: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 20 sides, plus a 100-sided percentile die.
